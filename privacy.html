Kitty City privacy policy

Last updated November 17, 2026. Made by demi rose. Questions: gardenr946@gmail.com

The short version

  • Kitty City is a game for children. There are no accounts, no ads, no purchases and no tracking.
  • We do not ask for a real name, an email address, a photo, a location, a microphone or a camera.
  • A child's game (kitty name, look, coins, furniture, friends) is saved on the child's own device. We do not receive it.
  • The game sends nothing about how a child plays. The only thing it can send is a few anonymous bug counts, described below, which a grown-up can turn off.
  • To play in the same town as a friend, the game sends the kitty's name, look and position to the other players in that town, in real time, straight between the devices, helped by free public connection services. We do not keep it.

What the game saves on the device

The kitty's name (letters only, up to 12, with a word filter), its look and outfits, coins, furniture and houses, pets, farm, mini-game levels, game settings, and a list of friends (each friend's kitty name and a random code). This stays on the device. A grown-up can make a backup file with the Backup button; the file goes wherever the grown-up chooses.

Playing together

A town is opened with a key made of four pictures. Everyone who enters the same key is in the same town. While they play, each device shares the kitty's name, look, position and what it is doing with the other players in that town. Friends can also send each other postcards; these are picked from pictures and are sent only to the friend.

Devices connect to each other directly (WebRTC). To find each other they use PeerJS's free public connection service (0.peerjs.com), and some connections go through free public relay servers (Google's, PeerJS's and Open Relay). Those services can see the internet address of a device that connects through them, and they do not receive the game's contents beyond what a relay carries. We do not run a server that keeps anything. A device that is playing in the same town as another can, as with any direct connection, be reached at its internet address by the other devices in that town.

Calls. Friends in the same town can call each other. The device asks for the microphone only when a call starts or is answered; the sound goes straight between the friends' devices and is not recorded or saved. A grown-up can turn calls off in Settings.

What we do not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising or analytics companies of any kind inside the game.
  • No tracking of children, and no sharing of any information with other companies.
  • Nothing in the game sends a child to another website or app.

Anonymous bug counts

Kitty City can send a few anonymous counts so the people who look after the game can find and fix bugs. The counts are: how many times an error happened and in which part of the game (never what a player typed), the spot in town where a kitty got stuck, a button that did nothing after quick taps, a sound or voice that did not start, how many seconds the game ran slowly, and whether the device could not save.

A message has no name, no kitty name, no friends, no chat, no town key, no account number, no device number, no location and no time of day. It is sent only when something went wrong; if nothing went wrong, nothing is sent. We use the counts only to fix bugs, and we keep only a summary of them.

The message is sent over the internet to ntfy.sh, a free public message service, and is read from there by the people who look after the game. Like every website, ntfy.sh can see the internet address a message came from; it uses it to stop abuse and, by its own policy, may keep it briefly. We never receive or keep it. ntfy.sh deletes messages after about 12 hours. A grown-up can turn this off in the Grown-ups screen (Bug counts).

Blocking and reporting

Tapping another player's name lets a child block that player or report them. A reported kitty is hidden right away, and a note of the report is kept on the device for a grown-up to see on the Grown-ups screen. Nothing is sent to us or to anyone else.

Children

Kitty City is made for children ages 4 to 8 (and younger children playing with a grown-up). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. We keep nothing about children, so there is nothing to review or delete on our side. The bug counts contain no personal information. Clearing the website's data in the browser removes the game from the device. A friend's own device may still hold your kitty's name in their friends list until they remove it.

Changes
